# Task

Compare the submitted search concepts to the research question and identify if the submitted
concepts are the best possible concepts to choose for carrying out a keyword search on a
bibliographic database. Refer to best practice in identifying search concepts when deciding if
they are suitable.

# Best practice

- Most searches use **two to four concepts**. These are usually the Population/Problem and the
  Intervention/Exposure. The Outcome is included only when it is specific and essential.
- The **Comparison is often left out**: terms like "placebo", "usual care" or "standard
  treatment" are described inconsistently in abstracts and make the search miss relevant papers.
- **But a comparator can be a valid concept** when the student wants papers about *either*
  treatment, not just head-to-head comparisons (e.g. triptans *or* NSAIDs for migraine). Then both
  are kept as concepts and combined with OR in stage 5. Check the student's notes for this, and
  if the intention is unclear, ask rather than telling them to remove it.
- **One idea per concept.** "Older adults with type 2 diabetes" contains two concepts
  (older adults; type 2 diabetes) that should be searched separately and combined later.
- **Leave out generic words** that add nothing searchable: "effect", "impact", "role",
  "treatment", "patients", "outcomes", "relationship", "management", "study".
- **Leave out study design words** ("randomised controlled trial", "systematic review") unless
  the student is deliberately applying a filter; a design limit is better added at the end.
- Concepts should be short noun phrases that authors would actually use in titles and abstracts.

# Items

Return one item per submitted concept, labelled with the concept exactly as the student typed
it. Use status:
- "strong" for a good concept,
- "developing" for one that should be narrowed, split, reworded or removed,
- and add an extra item labelled "Missing concept" with status "missing" if an essential idea in
  the question has no concept.

On early attempts, don't name the missing concept. Hint at which part of PICO it comes from.
