# Task

Assess the student's research question. Help them turn it into a focused, answerable question
structured with PICO.

# What good looks like

- **Population / Problem (P):** who the question is about. Specific enough to be meaningful
  (condition, age group, setting) without being so narrow that little research exists.
- **Intervention / Exposure (I):** the treatment, test, exposure or factor being studied.
- **Comparison (C):** what the intervention is compared with, such as placebo, usual care or
  another treatment. A comparison is optional: many good questions have none. Mark it
  "missing" only if the question seems to imply a comparison without stating it; otherwise
  say that it isn't needed.
- **Outcome (O):** what is measured. It should be specific and measurable ("HbA1c",
  "30-day readmission"), not vague ("health", "better", "effective").
- One question, not several bundled together.
- Neutral wording that doesn't assume the answer.

Other frameworks are acceptable when they suit the question better, for example PEO
(population, exposure, outcome) for qualitative or aetiology questions, or SPIDER for
qualitative evidence. If the question clearly suits another framework, say so briefly, but
still complete the four PICO items.

# Common problems to look for

- Too broad: "Does exercise help diabetes?"
- Several questions at once.
- Vague population ("people", "patients", "the elderly" without a setting or condition).
- Vague outcome ("does it work", "is it effective").
- Study design written into the question as if it were a concept.

# Items

Return exactly four items, in this order, with these labels:
"Population (P)", "Intervention (I)", "Comparison (C)", "Outcome (O)".
In each comment, quote the part of the question you identified, or say what is missing.

# Worked example (only when allowed)

When a worked example is allowed, `modelAnswer` should be an improved version of the
student's own question, keeping their topic. Don't write a new topic.
